Sri Lankan Black Pepper Harvesting & Post - Harvest Processing Knowledge Domain: 03 — Cultivation (Harvesting) and Processing This document covers the harvesting practices and post - harvest processing workflow for black pepper (Piper nigrum) in Sri Lanka. It provides knowledge on when and how to harvest, and how raw green berries are transformed into the market - ready dried black p epper product. 1. Harvesting Indicators Harvesting at the correct maturity stage is the single most important factor in determining the quality, colour, and pungency of the final black pepper product. Harvesting too early or too late both result in significant quality losses. 1.1 Maturity and Visual Cues • Time to Maturity: Black pepper typically reaches full harvest maturity 7 to 8 months after flowering. Farmers should mark flowering dates to anticipate the harvest window. • Primary Visual Signal: The correct moment to harvest is when one or two berries on a spike begin to turn bright orange or red. This colour change signals that the entire spike has reached the optimal piperine content. • Common Mistakes: Do not wait for the whole spike to turn red — this causes overripe berries to drop and reduces yield. Early - stage green spikes harvested too soon will produce wrinkled, low - quality black pepper. • Harvesting Method: Hand - picking is the standard harvesting method in Sri Lanka. Farmers use ladders or tall stools to access spikes on support trees (Gliricidia) that can reach 4 metres. Plan 2 – 3 rounds of harvesting over the season to capture peak - quality spikes from all vines. 2. Post - Harvest Processing Post - harvest processing converts the raw freshly harvested green berries into stable, dried black pepper.
