Storage Graded pepper Market - ready product Store in clean, dry, well - ventilated jute or polypropylene bags; moisture < 12% 2.2 Threshing (Spike Removal) Immediately after harvesting, the green berries must be separated from the spike stalks (rachis). Delayed threshing accelerates fermentation and spoilage. • Setup: Spread harvested spikes on a clean, dry cement floor or tarpaulin. • Method: Thresh manually by rubbing spikes between palms or feet, or use a mechanical thresher for larger volumes. • Cleanup: Collect and remove all rachis material after separation to avoid contamination of the final product. 2.3 Blanching — The Key to Premium Ceylon Black Pepper Blanching is an optional but highly recommended step for farmers targeting premium - grade or export - quality "Ceylon Black Pepper." It is the key differentiator in producing a uniformly black, visually appealing product. • Preparation: Fill a large vessel with clean water and heat to exactly 80°C (avoid boiling, which damages the berries). • Blanching Process: Immerse the separated green berries in the hot water for 1 to 2 minutes only. Over - blanching softens berries and damages quality. • After Blanching: Remove berries immediately and spread them on drying surfaces — blanched berries must not be left in standing water. Benefits of Blanching: 1. Produces a uniform, deep black colour across the entire batch — essential for export premium pricing. 2. Accelerates the subsequent drying process by breaking down the outer skin, allowing faster moisture escape. 3. Kills surface microorganisms and pathogens, making the final product safer and extending shelf life. Export Note: Blanched and properly dried Ceylon Black Pepper commands higher prices in international markets due to its consistent colour, lower microbial load, and superior appearance. 2.4 Drying Drying is the most critical post - harvest step for long - term quality preservation. Insufficient drying causes mould growth, mycotoxin contamination, and complete product loss. • Sun Drying Setup: Spread berries in a single layer on bamboo mats, clean cement floors, or black polyethylene sheets under direct sunlight. • Turning: Turn the berries every 2 – 3 hours to ensure uniform drying on all sides. • Duration: In Sri Lanka's tropical climate, sun drying typically takes 3 to 5 days to achieve the target moisture content. • Target Moisture: The final moisture content must be 12% or below to prevent mould growth and ensure quality is maintained during storage and transport. • Alternative: In wet or cloudy weather, mechanical
