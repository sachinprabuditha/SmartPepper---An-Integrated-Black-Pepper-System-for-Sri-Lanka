Plant at the onset of monsoon rains — this is the single most critical timing factor for successful establishment. 5. Select only 4 – 6 month old, hardened nursery plants with 5 – 8 healthy leaves and vigorous growth for field planting. 6. Plant the pepper vine 15 – 20 cm away from the base of the Gliricidia support stick — do not plant directly at the base. 7. Firm the soil around the root ball gently to eliminate air pockets without compacting the soil. 8. Immediately after planting, erect temporary shade using coconut fronds, shade cloth, or any suitable material to protect the young plant from direct sunlight. 9. Apply a layer of mulch (straw, coir, or Gliricidia cuttings) around the base of the vine in a 50 cm radius to conserve soil moisture and suppress weeds. Intercrop Note: The recommended spacing of 2.4 m × 2.4 m is suitable for both mono - crop plantations and intercropping with coconut. This spacing yields approximately 1,700 plants per hectare and allows sufficient light and air circulation for both crops. Planting Season Warning: Never plant pepper vines during dry periods. Without reliable soil moisture from monsoon rains, young transplants suffer severe water stress and high mortality. If planting outside the monsoon window is unavoidable, supplemental irrigation must be in place before planting.
