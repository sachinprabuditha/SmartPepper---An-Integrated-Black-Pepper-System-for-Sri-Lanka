Place in propagator. Propagator Period Weeks 1 – 4 Keep sealed in propagator to maintain high humidity for root development. No direct watering needed. Partial Opening Weeks 4 – 7 Partially open propagator for controlled watering and weeding. Begin acclimatisation. Propagator Removal Weeks 6 – 7 Gradually remove polythene cover over 2 – 3 weeks. Do not remove abruptly — stagger removal to avoid transplant shock. Shade House Hardening Weeks 7 – 12 Move plants to shade house. Practice progressive hardening by reducing shade over the final 3 – 4 weeks. Field - Ready Plants Month 4 – 6 Plants should have 5 – 8 healthy leaves and vigorous growth before field planting. Total nursery period: 4 – 6 months. Critical Point: The total nursery period must be 4 to 6 months. Removing plants too early (before they have 5 – 8 leaves and strong roots) results in high field mortality. Hardening in the final 3 – 4 weeks is essential before transplanting. 2.3 Hardening Process Hardening is the gradual acclimatisation of nursery plants to outdoor conditions before field planting: 1. After removing the propagator, place plants in a well - ventilated shade house. 2. Over 3 – 4 weeks, progressively reduce the shade level — start at 75% shade and reduce to 25% by the final week. 3. Plants are considered field - ready when they show vigorous growth, 5 – 8 fully developed leaves, and a firm root ball. 3. Support Systems for Pepper Vines Pepper vines are climbing plants that require a support structure to grow vertically. In Sri Lanka, live support trees are the standard practice, providing shade, structural support, and additional organic matter through leaf litter. 3.1 Live Support Trees Three species are used as live standards for pepper vines in Sri Lanka: Species Common Name Usage Notes Gliricidia sepium Gliricidia Primary / Most Common Fast - growing; nitrogen - fixing legume. Lopped leaves used as green mulch. Sticks: 3 – 5 cm diameter, 2.5 m length, planted 20 cm deep. Erythrina indica Dadap Secondary / Alternative Provides good shade and structural support. Commonly used in older plantations. Grevillea robusta Silky Oak Alternative Tall timber tree that provides durable long - term support and partial shade. 3.2 Gliricidia Support — Installation Guidelines • Stick Dimensions: Select Gliricidia sticks of 3 – 5 cm diameter and 2.5 m in length. • Planting Depth: Plant the Gliricidia stick at a depth of 20 cm at the corner of the planting pit, firmly embedded to withstand vine weight. • Lead Time — Critical: Gliricidia support sticks must be planted at least 6 months before the pepper vine is planted.
