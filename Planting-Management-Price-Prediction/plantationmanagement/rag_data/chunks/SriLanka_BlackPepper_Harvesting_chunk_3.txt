Drying Standards Reference: Parameter Standard / Guideline Drying Surface Bamboo mats, clean cement floors, or black polyethylene sheets Drying Duration (Sun) 3 to 5 days under direct tropical sunlight Target Moisture Content 12% or less (mandatory for safe storage and export) Artificial Drying (Alternative) Mechanical dryers at 50 – 60°C if weather is unfavourable Turning Frequency Every 2 – 3 hours to ensure even drying Warning: Never pile berries in thick heaps during drying — this traps heat and moisture, promoting rapid mould development. Quality Standards and Storage Once dried, black pepper must be cleaned, graded, and stored correctly to preserve quality until it reaches the buyer or export facility. 3.1 Cleaning and Sorting • Debris Removal: Manually or mechanically remove all stalks, dust, stones, and plant debris from the dried batch. • Berry Grading: Discard shrivelled, discoloured, or undersized berries as these reduce overall grade and market value. • Sizing: Grade the final product by size using mesh screens or sieves to separate bold, medium, and small berries for different market channels. 3.2 Storage Conditions • Bags: Store in clean, dry, well - ventilated jute or polypropylene bags. Avoid airtight plastic containers, which trap humidity. • Environment: Maintain storage room temperature below 30°C with relative humidity below 70%. • Stacking: Keep bags elevated off the floor on pallets to prevent moisture absorption from the ground. • Monitoring: Inspect stored pepper monthly for signs of mould, insect infestation, or unusual odour. DEA Guideline: The Department of Export Agriculture recommends testing moisture content with a calibrated moisture meter before bagging. Batches exceeding 12% moisture must be re - dried before storage.
