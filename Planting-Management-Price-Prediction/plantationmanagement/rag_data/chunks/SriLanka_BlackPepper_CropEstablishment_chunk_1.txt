Sri Lankan Black Pepper Crop Establishment Knowledge Domain: 01 — Planting Practices (Crop Establishment) Project: RAG Chat System for Sri Lankan Black Pepper Knowledge This document covers the full crop establishment process for black pepper (Piper nigrum) cultivation in Sri Lanka, from the selection and preparation of planting material through nursery management and field planting . 1. Planting Material Black pepper is propagated vegetatively using stem cuttings rather than by seed, ensuring genetic consistency and predictable yield performance. The quality of planting material is the foundation of a productive plantation. 1.1 Propagation Method • Standard Method: Black pepper is almost exclusively propagated vegetatively from stem cuttings — seed propagation is not used for commercial cultivation. • Cutting Source: For commercial cultivation, cuttings are taken from terminal stems (top growing tip of the vine) or from ground runners (rooted runners along the soil surface). • Lateral Cuttings: Cuttings taken from lateral branches will produce bush - type plants rather than climbing vines — this is generally not preferred for commercial plantations. 1.2 Mother Vine Selection The quality of cuttings is entirely dependent on the quality of the mother vine. Strict selection criteria must be applied: Selection Criterion Standard / Requirement Yield High - yielding vine with consistent production over multiple seasons Health Status Completely free from pest infestation and disease symptoms Vine Vigour Vigorous, healthy growth with strong climbing habit Lateral Branches Produces abundant lateral branches with short inter - nodal distances Spike Quality Long spikes with complete coverage by berries (no gaps) Berry Quality Bold, well - filled berries with good dry weight ratio Climate Tolerance Tolerant to the specific climatic conditions of the intended planting area Key Rule: Never take cuttings from a vine showing any signs of disease, pest damage, or poor vigour — these defects will carry into the new plants. 2. Nursery Management A structured nursery phase of 4 to 6 months is essential for producing field - ready plants. Rushing the nursery process is one of the most common causes of poor field establishment. 2.1 Nursery Media and Containers • Poly Bags: Use 250 - gauge polythene bags measuring 8 inches × 5 inches. • Growing Medium: Fill bags with a mixture of equal parts topsoil, cow dung, coarse sand, and coir dust. This blend provides good drainage, moisture retention, and organic nutrition. • Mix Quality: Ensure the mixture is well - mixed and free from soil - borne pathogens before use. 2.2 Nursery Timeline The nursery process follows a structured 6 - phase timeline from planting to field readiness: Nursery Phase Timing Action / Notes Planting in Poly Bags Week 1 Insert cuttings into 250 - gauge 8"×5" poly bags filled with topsoil, cow dung, sand, and coir dust (equal parts). Place in propagator. Propagator Period Weeks 1 – 4 Keep sealed in propagator to maintain high humidity for root development. No direct watering needed.
