Sri Lankan Black Pepper Harvesting & Post - Harvest Processing Knowledge Domain: 03 — Cultivation (Harvesting) and Processing This document covers the harvesting practices and post - harvest processing workflow for black pepper (Piper nigrum) in Sri Lanka. It provides knowledge on when and how to harvest, and how raw green berries are transformed into the market - ready dried black p epper product. 1. Harvesting Indicators Harvesting at the correct maturity stage is the single most important factor in determining the quality, colour, and pungency of the final black pepper product. Harvesting too early or too late both result in significant quality losses. 1.1 Maturity and Visual Cues • Time to Maturity: Black pepper typically reaches full harvest maturity 7 to 8 months after flowering. Farmers should mark flowering dates to anticipate the harvest window. • Primary Visual Signal: The correct moment to harvest is when one or two berries on a spike begin to turn bright orange or red. This colour change signals that the entire spike has reached the optimal piperine content. • Common Mistakes: Do not wait for the whole spike to turn red — this causes overripe berries to drop and reduces yield. Early - stage green spikes harvested too soon will produce wrinkled, low - quality black pepper. • Harvesting Method: Hand - picking is the standard harvesting method in Sri Lanka. Farmers use ladders or tall stools to access spikes on support trees (Gliricidia) that can reach 4 metres. Harvest Readiness Reference: Indicator Target Value Practical Notes Time from Flowering 7 to 8 months Monitor from first spike emergence Berry Color (Harvest Signal) 1 – 2 berries turn bright orange or red Do not wait for full spike ripening Berry Texture Firm, plump, fully developed Shrivelled or pale berries indicate premature harvest Spike Condition Majority of berries still green Green berries produce the best black pepper quality Tip: Stagger harvesting across the farm — not all vines ripen simultaneously. Plan 2 – 3 rounds of harvesting over the season to capture peak - quality spikes from all vines. 2. Post - Harvest Processing Post - harvest processing converts the raw freshly harvested green berries into stable, dried black pepper. Each step must be performed promptly and correctly to preserve quality, meet export standards, and prevent spoilage. 2.1 Full Processing Workflow The complete post - harvest processing chain from spike to market is summarised below: Processing Step Input Output Key Action / Notes 1. Storage Graded pepper Market - ready product Store in clean, dry, well - ventilated jute or polypropylene bags; moisture < 12% 2.2 Threshing (Spike Removal) Immediately after harvesting, the green berries must be separated from the spike stalks (rachis). Delayed threshing accelerates fermentation and spoilage. • Setup: Spread harvested spikes on a clean, dry cement floor or tarpaulin. • Method: Thresh manually by rubbing spikes between palms or feet, or use a mechanical thresher for larger volumes. • Cleanup: Collect and remove all rachis material after separation to avoid contamination of the final product. 2.3 Blanching — The Key to Premium Ceylon Black Pepper Blanching is an optional but highly recommended step for farmers targeting premium - grade or export - quality "Ceylon Black Pepper." It is the key differentiator in producing a uniformly black, visually appealing product. • Preparation: Fill a large vessel with clean water and heat to exactly 80°C (avoid boiling, which damages the berries). • Blanching Process: Immerse the separated green berries in the hot water for 1 to 2 minutes only. Over - blanching softens berries and damages quality. • After Blanching: Remove berries immediately and spread them on drying surfaces — blanched berries must not be left in standing water. Benefits of Blanching: 1. Produces a uniform, deep black colour across the entire batch — essential for export premium pricing. 2. Accelerates the subsequent drying process by breaking down the outer skin, allowing faster moisture escape. 3. Kills surface microorganisms and pathogens, making the final product safer and extending shelf life. Export Note: Blanched and properly dried Ceylon Black Pepper commands higher prices in international markets due to its consistent colour, lower microbial load, and superior appearance. 2.4 Drying Drying is the most critical post - harvest step for long - term quality preservation. Insufficient drying causes mould growth, mycotoxin contamination, and complete product loss. • Sun Drying Setup: Spread berries in a single layer on bamboo mats, clean cement floors, or black polyethylene sheets under direct sunlight. • Turning: Turn the berries every 2 – 3 hours to ensure uniform drying on all sides. • Duration: In Sri Lanka's tropical climate, sun drying typically takes 3 to 5 days to achieve the target moisture content. • Target Moisture: The final moisture content must be 12% or below to prevent mould growth and ensure quality is maintained during storage and transport. • Alternative: In wet or cloudy weather, mechanical dryers operating at 50 – 60°C can be used as an alternative to sun drying. Drying Standards Reference: Parameter Standard / Guideline Drying Surface Bamboo mats, clean cement floors, or black polyethylene sheets Drying Duration (Sun) 3 to 5 days under direct tropical sunlight Target Moisture Content 12% or less (mandatory for safe storage and export) Artificial Drying (Alternative) Mechanical dryers at 50 – 60°C if weather is unfavourable Turning Frequency Every 2 – 3 hours to ensure even drying Warning: Never pile berries in thick heaps during drying — this traps heat and moisture, promoting rapid mould development. A single - layer spread is mandatory for safe drying. 3. Quality Standards and Storage Once dried, black pepper must be cleaned, graded, and stored correctly to preserve quality until it reaches the buyer or export facility. 3.1 Cleaning and Sorting • Debris Removal: Manually or mechanically remove all stalks, dust, stones, and plant debris from the dried batch. • Berry Grading: Discard shrivelled, discoloured, or undersized berries as these reduce overall grade and market value. • Sizing: Grade the final product by size using mesh screens or sieves to separate bold, medium, and small berries for different market channels. 3.2 Storage Conditions • Bags: Store in clean, dry, well - ventilated jute or polypropylene bags. Avoid airtight plastic containers, which trap humidity. • Environment: Maintain storage room temperature below 30°C with relative humidity below 70%. • Stacking: Keep bags elevated off the floor on pallets to prevent moisture absorption from the ground. • Monitoring: Inspect stored pepper monthly for signs of mould, insect infestation, or unusual odour. DEA Guideline: The Department of Export Agriculture recommends testing moisture content with a calibrated moisture meter before bagging. Batches exceeding 12% moisture must be re - dried before storage.
