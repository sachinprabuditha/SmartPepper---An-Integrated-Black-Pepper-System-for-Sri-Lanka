Research Domain

Domain & Background

Exploring the literature, identifying gaps, and defining the research scope for Sri Lanka's black pepper industry.

Literature Survey

Black pepper (Piper nigrum) is grown primarily in Sri Lanka's Matale, Kandy, Matara and Hambantota districts. Despite its economic significance, the sector relies on largely manual processes across quality assessment, disease diagnosis, price discovery and export documentation.

Prior research on agricultural quality grading used handcrafted color histogram, texture and shape features for machine vision. Deep convolutional networks (AlexNet, VGG, ResNet) later improved accuracy for grading coffee, rice, wheat and spices. However, existing spice grading systems operate at batch level and have not integrated the internationally standardized Gram-Liter density measurement (ISO 959-1) with visual seed-level classification in a single automated pipeline.

Plant disease classification matured rapidly following Mohanty et al.'s demonstration of CNN effectiveness on the PlantVillage dataset. Lightweight architectures (MobileNet, EfficientNet, ResNet) enabled mobile-device deployment. For black pepper specifically, studies have analyzed Footrot (Quick wilt), Pollu (anthracnose) and Slow Decline via image classification, but none combined YOLOv8 instance segmentation for individual leaf isolation under natural field conditions with severity scoring and geospatial mapping.

Price prediction literature has employed SVR, Random Forests, LSTM networks and ensemble methods. Liyanage and Silva's stacked gradient boosting approach for cinnamon price forecasting in Sri Lanka provides the methodological foundation. No published study addresses district-grade-stratified black pepper price prediction in Sri Lanka with external macroeconomic variables (USD/LKR exchange rate).

Blockchain applications in agri-food supply chains have demonstrated provenance verification and payment settlement using Ethereum and Hyperledger Fabric. However, a live digital auction with blockchain escrow and automated export compliance on a single spice export platform has not been documented previously.

Research Gap

Despite significant activity in individual sub-domains, a vertically integrated platform addressing all four systemic challenges in Sri Lanka's black pepper sector does not exist. Four specific gaps motivated this research:


Quality Grading Gap

No existing system integrates ISO 959-1 Gram-Liter density measurement with per-seed visual defect classification in a single automated, low-cost pipeline for black pepper.

Disease Detection Gap

Previous black pepper disease systems require single manually-cropped leaf inputs. No solution uses YOLOv8 instance segmentation on full-vine field images with GPS-based spread mapping for Sri Lankan conditions.

Price Forecasting Gap

Black pepper price prediction in Sri Lanka is severely understudied. No district-grade-stratified model incorporates macroeconomic variables (USD/LKR rate) with a hallucination-safe RAG advisory chatbot.

Blockchain Auction Gap

No documented system combines live digital auction, smart-contract escrow settlement, and automated export compliance validation on a single high-value spice export platform.

Research Problem

Sri Lanka's black pepper industry faces four critical and interconnected technological deficits that collectively suppress farmer income, reduce product quality, facilitate certification fraud, and create information asymmetry that disadvantages smallholder producers:


Biosafety & Disease Risk

Diseases including Footrot (Quick Wilt), Pollu (Anthracnose) and Slow Decline go undetected until catastrophic crop loss. Diagnosis relies on farmer experience or slow laboratory analysis, with no early warning or geospatial tracking system in place.

Subjective Quality Grading

Pepper quality assessment remains manually conducted, resulting in inter-assessor variability, disputed grading outcomes, and inconsistent compliance with international export standards such as ISO 959-1.

Price Information Asymmetry

Market prices are transmitted through informal broker channels, leaving farmers without access to real-time or predictive price data. High-quality yields are systematically undersold due to information gaps and middlemen intermediation.

Supply Chain Opacity

Export documentation is paper-based with no standardized digital traceability. Certification fraud is difficult to detect and the absence of tamper-proof records prevents Sri Lankan pepper from meeting modern sustainability and transparency standards demanded by international buyers.

Research Objectives

SmartPepper aims to address each identified gap through four module-specific objectives that together form a vertically integrated solution:


  • 01
    Develop an IoT-enabled automated quality grading system combining Gram-Liter density measurement (load-cell) with ResNet18-based per-seed visual defect classification to replace manual inspection with a standardized, low-cost pipeline.
  • 02
    Build a mobile-first disease detection module using YOLOv8 instance segmentation and ResNet18 multi-class classification to identify four disease conditions at the individual leaf level, with severity scoring, spread prediction, and GPS-based geospatial farm mapping.
  • 03
    Create a district-grade-stratified price forecasting engine using a Stacked Boost Forest ensemble (Random Forest + Gradient Boosting + Linear Regression meta-learner), achieving MAE < 100 LKR/kg, paired with a RAG-based agronomy chatbot with an explicit Answerability Gate.
  • 04
    Implement a transparent, decentralized auction and export compliance platform on Ethereum using Solidity smart contracts, IPFS document storage, and QR/NFC-based Digital Pepper Passports to eliminate intermediary price opacity and certification fraud.

Methodology


Module 1 - IoT Automated Quality Grading

An ESP32 microcontroller orchestrates four sequential stations: a load-cell density station computing Gram-Liter (g/L = sample weight / cup volume), a conveyor motor system, a controlled-illumination imaging station with a mobile phone camera, and a wireless data transmission layer. ResNet18, pre-trained on ImageNet and fine-tuned on domain-specific pepper images, classifies each segmented seed as Normal, Moulded, or Discoloured. A rule-based decision engine combines density and visual results to assign a final export-grade. Hardware is managed through a web control interface, eliminating the need for physical tethering.

Module 2 - Mobile Disease Detection

Farmers photograph pepper vines via a Flutter mobile app. YOLOv8-seg performs instance segmentation to isolate individual leaf instances from complex multi-leaf field images. A fine-tuned ResNet18 classifies each segmented leaf into: Footrot (Quick Wilt), Pollu (Anthracnose), Slow Decline, or Healthy. Severity % = (infected leaves / total leaves) × 100. A spread prediction algorithm estimates time to full vine infection using disease-specific progression coefficients. Dual-path treatment recommendations (eco-friendly for low severity; targeted chemical protocols for high severity) are generated. GPS coordinates are captured simultaneously to populate a farm-level geomap.

Module 3 - Price Forecasting & RAG Chatbot

A three-stage Stacked Boost Forest ensemble is trained on 2016–2025 weekly purchase prices (Dept. of Export Agriculture), stratified by district (Matale, Kandy, Matara, Hambantota) and pepper grade, with the weekly USD/LKR exchange rate as an external feature. Stage 1: Random Forest (non-linear feature interactions). Stage 2: Gradient Boosting on Stage-1 residuals. Stage 3: Linear Regression meta-learner. Income projection uses Dried Yield = Fresh Yield × Drying Ratio and Income = Dried Yield × Expected Price. The RAG chatbot uses OpenAI Embeddings, Qdrant vector search, and an Answerability Gate to ensure only expert-validated agronomic knowledge is delivered.

Module 4 - Blockchain Auction & Export Compliance

Solidity smart contracts on Ethereum govern auction rules (minimum bid increment, duration) and escrow lifecycle (deposit → compliance approval + shipment confirmation → release). Lot provenance, auction outcomes and compliance events are written to an immutable ledger; IPFS stores certification documents via content-addressed hashes. Each lot receives a Digital Pepper Passport accessible via QR code or NFC tag. Firebase Realtime Database provides sub-second WebSocket bid propagation for the live auction interface. A rule-based compliance engine validates packaging, labelling and certification at pre-auction listing and pre-shipment checkpoints.

About Us

SmartPepper is a final-year research project by a team of Software Engineering undergraduates at SLIIT.

We are a team of four Software Engineering students from the Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology (SLIIT), Malabe. Our research tackles the digital transformation challenges faced by Sri Lanka's black pepper industry through an interdisciplinary approach combining IoT hardware, mobile computing, machine learning, and blockchain technology. The project is conducted under the guidance of our supervisors, Ms. Hansi de Silva and Ms. Ishara Weerathunga.
